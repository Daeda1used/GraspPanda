    Does it train like the shipped component?¶

    An option that runs (Does it run?) may still train badly. Here each swap that ran trains 250 iterations beside the method as shipped (the reference below, not the authors' code), and the losses are compared term by term. Other pages call this check trains like shipped.

    A probe shows that a combination trains on real frames of GraspNet-1Billion, not how good it is. Accuracy is claimed only for a method trained and evaluated here.

    One run per cell, 250 iterations on 8 frames of one scene. An outcome is about training dynamics, not an accuracy measurement: a cell on a par may still detect worse grasps, and one that is worse here may catch up over a full schedule.

    How a cell is judged¶

    • The same settings for a cell and its reference: 250 iterations on 8 RealSense frames of GraspNet-1Billion (one scene), seed 0, at a constant learning rate (FineGrasp: its own warm-up), beside the method as shipped trained the same way twice.
    • Per loss term, the mean of the last 25 iterations of the cell is divided by the reference's.
    • On a par: every comparable term ends within the method's tolerance, nothing rises, every value is finite and a frame has a grasp. Worse: a term ends above the tolerance, or a part got no gradient. Diverging: a term rises at the end, a value became non-finite, or no grasp was predicted.
    • A term the option redefines is not compared (a loss preset, a head bundle, an assigner, a stage with its own loss code); the cell is judged on the other terms.
    • Counted only where the comparison holds: a swap that did not run at level 1, and a cell trained on a schedule other than its reference's, are not counted; Not on this page names them.
    • The tolerance of a method is 1.25 unless its two reference runs (seed 0 and 1) differ by more; then it is the next step of 0.05 above that spread.
    • FineGrasp runs under --schedule recipe, its reference and its cells alike: at the constant 0.002 without the recipe's 800-iteration warm-up, the two post-norm transformer layers that fuse FineGrasp's four crop scales give every seed one feature (the reference implementation's fusion does the same), so the head learns the label distribution and every grasp gets one score until about iteration 530; under the warm-up the fusion recovers by iteration 80.
    Thresholds, measures and outcomes in full
    • mean: the mean of a loss term over the last 25 of the 250 iterations (reported, finite values; the total loss is not judged, it sums terms whose definitions may differ).
    • ratio: the cell's mean divided by the reference's mean of the same term.
    • rise: over the last half of the run (iterations 126 to 250), the mean of its second half divided by the mean of its first half, minus 1 (the middle iteration of an odd count in neither half): +0.30 means the term ended 30 % higher than it was half-way.
    • tolerance: per method: 1.25, or the first multiple of 0.05 above the largest ratio between two seeds of its reference (any term but the total, either way round), when that is larger.
    • rising: a rise above the rising threshold of the method that also exceeds the reference's rise of the term by more than it (the rise alone for a term the reference does not report); the threshold is 0.1, or the first multiple of 0.05 above the largest difference between the rises of one term under two seeds of the reference, when that is larger.
    • lower: a comparable term below 1/tolerance of the reference's: reported, not judged.

    Outcomes, in order of precedence:

    Outcome Meaning
    fails the run did not finish: an exception, a crash, a timeout, or no real frame read. A run that died of GPU memory is not recorded (it says nothing about the config) and is run again
    no reference the method as shipped has no finished run under the same settings: nothing to compare with
    diverging a loss term is rising over the last half of the run (see rising), a loss or a weight became non-finite, or no grasp was predicted on any of the 4 frames while the reference predicts some
    worse not diverging, and a comparable loss term ends above the tolerance of the method times the reference's (mean of the last 25 iterations), or a part of the model that holds trainable parameters received no gradient in any iteration
    not comparable not diverging, nothing worse, but the cell changes the definition of every term the reference has: there is no term to compare
    on a par every comparable loss term ends within the tolerance of the method, at least one term is comparable, nothing rises, every value is finite and a frame has a grasp. A term far below the reference's is reported (lower), not judged

    A term is not comparable when the cell changes its definition; the cell is judged on the others:

    • loss: a loss preset replaces the function of the term it is put in.
    • head: a head bundle defines the targets and the loss of every head term (score, width, angle, depth, tolerance).
    • assigner: an assigner changes the labels the view stage and the head see (a view stage sees the labels through the assigner only): every view and head term.
    • stage code: a proposal or view stage owns its targets and its loss: one whose class computes them with other code (its own loss or target methods, not inherited from the reference's stage) defines every term of that stage anew; one that computes them as the reference's does, with the same loss options, keeps them (a proposal that changes only how it samples).
    • presence: a term only one of the two runs reports.
    • sign: a term whose mean is not positive (a weighted loss can go below zero).

    The tolerance and the rising threshold of a method. A cell and its reference are two runs, so a ratio away from 1 means something only beyond what two runs of the very same config show. Every reference was therefore trained twice, under seed 0 and seed 1 (The noise floor). Under the five seed-based methods, a term that has settled (it rose or fell by less than 10 % over the last half) agrees within 7 % between the seeds; a term still falling steeply at the end differs more (EconomicGrasp's graspness by 1.32x), and HGGD at its peak rate differs by up to 1.55x (angle, offsets). One tolerance for all would be blind under the stable methods or trigger-happy under HGGD, so each method gets its own: 1.25 (the plan's value, more than three times the spread of a settled term) unless its references spread more, then the first multiple of 0.05 above their largest ratio. The rising threshold follows the same rule from the rises. The spread comes from one pair of runs per method: a third seed would firm it up.

    GSNet 1.088 1.25 0.192 0.2
    Scale-Balanced-Grasp 1.178 1.25 0.055 0.1

    Not on this page¶

    • Post-processing: post-processing trains nothing, so a training run says nothing about it; its check is to compare the grasps in and out of the chain (and the evaluator) on 20 frames of a trained method, which is not part of this page yet.
    • Sets of stages, and requests that replace several slots: several slots replaced at once: no single stage of the reference to compare with.
    • Schedules (42 cells): level 2 holds the schedule of the method as shipped fixed (a constant rate; FineGrasp: its own warm-up), so a schedule swap is not compared.
    • On a schedule of its own (30 cells): it trained on a schedule of its own, not on that of the method as shipped (a schedule-free optimizer, a recipe preset with a scheduler).
    • Did not run at level 1 (23 cells): the swap did not run at level 1; it stays named on every page.
    • Several loss terms at once (11 cells): a loss preset is read from its cells of one term.
    • The method's own component (1 cell): it is the method as shipped, not a swap.
