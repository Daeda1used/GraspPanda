    Does it run?¶

    Which option trains on real frames under which method? Every option was composed under each method it fits; those accepted were probed on real frames. The few without a record of their own (two adapters compose inserts, two point-painting wrappers, post=target-mask and loss=distribution-focal, measured inside decoupled-grid-bins), the refusals and the options that need your consent are named per option.

    A probe shows that a combination trains on real frames of GraspNet-1Billion, not how good it is. Accuracy is claimed only for a method trained and evaluated here.

    How it was judged¶

    • One cell, one option under one method: grasppanda compose <method config> --set <slot>=<option>, then grasppanda probe on the composed config. Measured on: 20 training iterations on 8 RealSense frames of GraspNet-1Billion (one scene), seed 0, one run per cell; nothing was measured on GraspClutter6D or on Kinect frames.
    • Four levels, each needing the one before: composes (config accepted), runs (trains on real frames without an error), learns (its losses fall), predicts (it ranks grasps). Exact definitions.
    • A refusal is the config check's answer: nothing was built, and the kind says why (refusal words).
    • Undecided counts against: a method where a level could not be decided stays in the count, named with the reason.
    • One run per cell: the config hash and the first losses repeat exactly; later numbers may differ in the next run.

    1221 of the 1262 records on this page were judged by judge 2 (judge: 2 in the records), which also records whether the parts of a model learned: the trend of every loss term, the units no gradient reached, the parts that did not change and the spread of the scores. A record of the first judge has none of it, so its learns and predicts are undecided (not checked). The loss of every iteration of every probe of judge 2 is in measured_combinations/losses_per_iteration.json.

    The flags of a loss term, named in the tails (active iterations: the term was reported, finite and not exactly 0):

    • flat: the term was active in at least two iterations, and the mean of its last quarter differs from its first active value by less than 5 % of that value.
    • rising: the term was active in at least two iterations, and the mean of the last half of its active iterations exceeds the mean of the first half by more than 10 % of the first half's mean (with an odd count the middle iteration is in neither half).
    • zero_at_end: the term is exactly 0.0, or not reported, at the last completed iteration.
    • constant: the term was reported in at least two iterations with the very same value in every one of them, zeros included.

    The schedules of a probe (schedule in a record):

    • recipe: the config's own scheduler, run over the probe. The probe sets train.epochs to ceil(iterations / iterations per epoch), so a schedule that steps per epoch is compressed into those few epochs (GSNet's 0.95 per epoch ends at 0.95^9 = 0.63 after 20 iterations of 2 per epoch, EconomicGrasp's cosine at 2.4 % of its start) and one that steps per iteration runs its first steps only (FineGrasp's warm-up of 800 iterations keeps a probe of 20 at no more than 2.5 % of its rate).
    • constant: no scheduler: every parameter group of every optimizer trains at the recipe's peak rate for the whole probe. The peak is the rate the optimizer gives the group (the configured lr, with the multipliers of ParamGroups), except for a schedule that sets a peak of its own: max_lr of OneCycleLR and CyclicLR, the larger factor of LinearLR. Every other schedule of the toolbox starts at that rate or reaches it at the end of its warm-up. When the probe's batches hold fewer frames than the recipe's, the peak is scaled by their ratio (the linear scaling rule: FineGrasp's 0.008 for 32 frames trains at 0.002 on batches of 8). A batch-norm momentum that the recipe decays per epoch (BNMomentumHook: GraspNet-baseline, Scale-Balanced-Grasp, HGGD) is held at its first epoch's value likewise (hooks.<i>.decay=1): decayed over epochs of a few iterations it can reach its floor of 0.001 within the probe (Scale-Balanced-Grasp's at iteration 73), and the running statistics, which prediction normalises with, then stop following the weights; training itself does not read them. The optimizer (its weight decay and its momentum as configured), clipping, accumulation and every other hook are the recipe's.
