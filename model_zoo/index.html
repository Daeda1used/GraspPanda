    Model zoo¶

    Every number on this page comes from a result record of a whole test split, scored by the evaluator of this toolbox and recomputed by a test from its per-scene values. AP is in percent; Filter says whether each method's own collision filter was off or on.

    • Authors' weights scored here (author-ckpt): GraspNet-baseline, Scale-Balanced-Grasp, EconomicGrasp, FineGrasp, HGGD. Those numbers have the provenance author-ckpt; they are not reproductions.
    • Trained here (reproduced): GraspNet-baseline, GSNet, Scale-Balanced-Grasp, EconomicGrasp, FineGrasp and HGGD, from scratch with the recipe of their configs. GraspNet-baseline is within the admission gate on every split. Scale-Balanced-Grasp and EconomicGrasp land on their authors' checkpoints, and with them outside the gate; GSNet is above its paper by more than 1 AP on every split, FineGrasp below it. HGGD is 8 to 10 AP below its authors' checkpoint on seen: the labels its released converter writes are not the ones that checkpoint fits, and on denser labels it lands on the checkpoint. Their weights are not published yet, so the zoo cannot download them.

    What to know when reading it
    • Inputs derived from ground truth. GraspNet-baseline, GSNet, Scale-Balanced-Grasp, EconomicGrasp and FineGrasp crop the workspace with the ground-truth segmentation at test time, as their references do; their records are stamped gt_assisted: workspace. HGGD reads the whole image and nothing in its input is derived from ground truth (gt_assisted is empty). The two kinds of line are not like-for-like.
    • The filter of each reference. off is the prediction as emitted (variant raw), on the model-free collision filter of each method's reference (variant cd), which is not one setting: voxels of 0.005 m for EconomicGrasp and 0.01 m for the others, and for HGGD what its released test script dumps, its own filter followed by NMS. The chain with all its parameters is in each record.
    • Reported numbers without a filter. The HGGD lines not stated are, for each camera, the "In paper" and "In repo" rows of its README, in that order. The GraspNet-baseline line is the paper's number as its repository README quotes it ("In paper"); the README says of the "In repo" row only that it adds collision detection. EconomicGrasp's reported numbers are read as filter off: neither its README nor the paper says, and the shipped test command leaves the filter off.
    • One run each. Every number is one run with seed 0. The spread over point-sampling seeds, and for HGGD over its random draws at inference, was not measured on whole splits.
    • Empty cells. GSNet has no author-ckpt number: the file released with its reference, a third-party implementation, is no reference for the benchmark (configs/gsnet/README.md). GraspNet-baseline, Scale-Balanced-Grasp and FineGrasp have lines for RealSense only.
    The models trained here: the admission gate, and what is known about each gap
    • Against the admission gate. The gate asks for at most 1.0 AP between reproduced and reported on each split. GraspNet-baseline meets it, with the collision filter: the one setting its repository reports. EconomicGrasp meets it on similar and novel and is 0.13 AP outside it on seen; Scale-Balanced-Grasp meets it on novel only. GSNet and FineGrasp are outside it on every split, and neither gap is explained. HGGD has no pair to hold against it: neither of its reported rows states the collision filter.
    • GraspNet-baseline is a little below its authors' checkpoint: −0.87 / −1.30 / −0.23 AP from it without the filter and −1.09 / −0.80 / −0.23 with it. Whether that is the spread between trainings was not measured.
    • Scale-Balanced-Grasp lands on its authors' checkpoint: −0.55 / −0.54 / −0.14 AP from it without the filter, −0.29 / −0.53 / −0.25 with it. The checkpoint is itself 1.2 to 1.7 AP below the paper on seen and similar, so the gap to the paper is not one of training; object-balanced sampling, which the paper describes, is not implemented (configs/scale_balanced_grasp/README.md).
    • HGGD is far below its authors' checkpoint: −10.35 / −6.53 / −3.20 AP from it without the filter, −8.42 / −4.31 / −2.46 with it. The training step is the reference's, and the labels are those the released converter writes; the checkpoint does not fit those labels and fits denser ones. Trained on labels suppressed at 1 cm and 30° instead of 2 cm and 60°, HGGD ends −0.53 / +0.58 / +0.63 AP from the checkpoint without the filter and −0.11 / +1.40 / +0.79 with it (projects/hggd_labels; its line here stays the training on the converter's labels).
    • EconomicGrasp lands on its authors' checkpoint: +0.06 / −0.09 / +1.04 AP from it without the filter, +0.22 / −0.02 / +1.01 with it. That checkpoint is itself 1.07 AP above the reported number on seen, so what separates the training here from the published table is what separates the authors' own file from it. Why the table is lower is not known (configs/economicgrasp/README.md).
    • GSNet is above the paper on every split, most on similar. What is known: this implementation equals the public reference implementation stage by stage, including every loss term of a recorded training batch, so its training step is the reference's; a model trained independently with the reference code, on a modified graspness label set, also scores above the paper; and the paper's numbers come from the authors' original code, which is not public (configs/gsnet/README.md).
    • FineGrasp is below the paper on every split, most on similar. What is known: the authors' weights score the paper's numbers through the same test path, and a recorded training batch gives the reference's losses and gradients. The authors' normal images and graspness labels are not published, so a training here generates both from the paper's equations, and it ran as four processes with accumulated gradients in place of the eight of the published recipe (configs/finegrasp/README.md).
    • One seed. GSNet and EconomicGrasp were trained three times with it; a line is the first training. GSNet's three lie within 1.12 AP of one another on every split, EconomicGrasp's within 1.75. The other four were trained once. Other seeds were not trained, nor was a training with unmodified reference code run. Each training run is described in its method's README.
    • The weights are not published yet. They were trained on GraspNet-1Billion (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), and whether and where they are published has not been decided. The metafile lists them with their sha256 and no url, so the zoo, GraspInferencer and grasppanda demo say that they are not published instead of downloading anything; the training log and the run snapshot are not published either.

    What to know when reading it
    • Inputs derived from ground truth. The five seed-based methods crop the workspace to the image rectangle around the ground-truth segmentation, the crop of the official GraspClutter6D tool (gt_assisted: workspace). It is not the crop the weights were trained with; what that costs was measured on a subset only, see Cross-dataset evaluation. HGGD reads the whole image (gt_assisted is empty) and its on lines add NMS to the filter, as on GraspNet-1Billion: its lines are not like-for-like with the others.
    • Which author-ckpt line the reported number is close to differs by method: on for GraspNet-baseline (0.81 AP below the reported value; off is 9.25 below) and for Scale-Balanced-Grasp (0.00 apart; off is 0.93 below), off for EconomicGrasp (0.02 above; on is 0.58 above). Whether the paper applied a collision filter for some methods and not for others was not investigated and cannot be read from the paper.
    • FineGrasp knows its camera distance. Its encoder reads the coordinates of every point, and this split lies farther from the camera than any training cloud (0.75 m in the median against 0.42 m). With the depth coordinate of the features moved to the training distance, and nothing else changed, the same weights score 24.39 AP without the filter and 25.02 with it, and the model trained here does alike; the lines above are the method as published (its README).
    • One run each, with seed 0, and one training with one seed behind every reproduced number, as above. The paper has no row for GSNet, FineGrasp or HGGD.
    • Whether a model trained here transfers like its authors' checkpoint differs by method. Scale-Balanced-Grasp is within 0.14 AP of its checkpoint, with and without the filter. EconomicGrasp is 1.55 AP above its checkpoint without the filter and 1.47 with it; GraspNet-baseline is 1.26 AP below without and 3.05 below with it. On GraspNet-1Billion each of the three differs from its checkpoint by about 1 AP at most. HGGD, 8 to 10 AP below its checkpoint there, is 1.29 AP below it here without the filter and 0.07 with it. One training each: how much of this a second training would keep was not measured.
    • The subset numbers of the user guide (other cameras, other crops, the Kinect weights) were measured on 24 scenes and are not benchmark numbers.

    Admission gate¶

    A method enters only with all of: config, weights with sha256, training log, a result record produced by the pinned evaluator, and the run snapshot.

    Gate: |reproduced − reported| ≤ 1.0 AP on each of seen / similar / novel. If the gate fails the entry may still be published, with the gap, the author-ckpt number and the diagnosed cause stated. Results with and without collision filtering are always separate rows.

    Provenance, and where the records are

    Every number carries its provenance, and provenances are never mixed in one column:

    Provenance Meaning
    reported Taken from the paper or the authors' repository.
    author-ckpt The authors' released weights, evaluated in this toolbox.
    reproduced Trained and evaluated in this toolbox.

    A measured number is shown only when a result record backs it. The record of every author-ckpt and reproduced row is committed under configs/<method>/records/ and named by the row of configs/<method>/metafile.yaml; it holds the per-scene values, the coverage, the post-processing chain, the inputs derived from ground truth, the config hash, the code commit and the sha256 of the weights. A test recomputes every measured number of the tables above from those per-scene values.

    Two directories beside it hold whole-split records that no line of this page states: repeats/, the config of a zoo row trained again (GSNet, EconomicGrasp), and variants/, the weights of a zoo row with one option changed (FineGrasp's features on GraspClutter6D). The method's README states their numbers, and a test holds the two together.

    Recording a result¶

    Two commands turn a finished test run into a row of this page.

    The commands, and what the second one checks 
    grasppanda test <config> --weights <checkpoint> --opts runtime.work_dir=<work directory>
python -m tools.record_result <work directory> --method <directory below configs/>

    The second command refuses a run that did not score every frame of the whole test split, that ran on uncommitted code, or whose weights cannot be tied to the file the metafile lists (for a converted checkpoint: the file it was converted from, which the converter notes in the converted file). Otherwise it copies the result records to configs/<method>/records/<model>_<provenance>_<split>_<variant>.json, with the directories of the machine replaced by the name of their environment variable, and prints the rows to paste into the metafile, the numbers computed from the per-scene values. Run again, it changes nothing and says whether the metafile states the rows. The table lines of this page and of the method's README are then written by hand; the tests name the lines that are missing.

    While the metafile has no weights entry for the model, say what the weights are with --provenance author-ckpt or --provenance reproduced; the tool then prints the entry to complete. Weights trained here may be listed before they are published: the entry then has url: null, license: null and a short note saying that they are not published yet, beside their sha256.
