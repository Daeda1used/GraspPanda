    Compose and probe¶

    Goal: put another option into a slot of a method, check in about a minute that the combination trains on real frames, then train it.

    grasppanda compose <method config> --list <slot>
grasppanda compose <method config> --set <slot>=<option> -o my.yaml
grasppanda probe my.yaml
grasppanda train my.yaml

    A probe shows that a combination trains on real frames of GraspNet-1Billion, not how good it is. Accuracy is claimed only for a method trained and evaluated here.

    You need: GraspPanda installed. Steps 1 and 2 need no dataset. Step 3 reads the split sample of the config's dataset with the assets of the method (What a probe needs); without them it falls back to a wiring check and says so. The explorer does steps 1 and 2 in a browser and shows what was measured for every selection.

    1. See the options of a slot¶

    grasppanda compose <method config> --list backbone

    With a method config, compose lists the options of that method's slot, each with what was measured for it under that method, and the options that do not fit with the reason. Without a slot it lists every slot of the method; without a method config, every option of the slot. grasppanda compose --show backbone=ptv3 prints one option with its notes. Which slots a method has is on the Components page: the scene-encoder and stage slots exist in the five seed-based methods, the image encoder in HGGD, post-processing and the training recipe in all six.

    Options, presets and components

    An option is a preset or the name of a registered class. A preset is the part of a config a newcomer cannot guess: the settings of one component for table-top depth clouds of GraspNet-1Billion (grid size, radii, widths, sampling rules) and, where its stage reads a label asset, the dataset section that serves it. Its header says what must be re-tuned and why: GraspClutter6D objects are farther from the camera (0.70 m against 0.44 m), so lengths may need re-tuning there. Every scene encoder has one, because the constructor defaults of most encoders are those of room-scale benchmarks: a bare {type: PTv3} works on a 2 cm grid with six input channels.

    A class named by its type (--set grouper=CylinderGroup) is built with its constructor defaults, which grasppanda components <type> lists. Presets live in grasppanda/presets/<slot>/<name>.yaml. They are config fragments, not configs, and are not inherited: compose writes their values into the child config. In a wheel the measured records come with the package; in a checkout they are read from docs/advanced_guides/measured_combinations.json.

    2. Compose¶

    Pick a method; each example puts one option into one slot:

    grasppanda compose graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e \
    --set backbone=ptv3 -o baseline_ptv3.yaml

    PTv3 returns a feature for every point, and GraspNet-baseline takes the encoder's subsample as its seeds: compose wraps PTv3 in the adapter FurthestPointSubsample.

    grasppanda compose gsnet/gsnet_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e \
    --set grouper=roi-grid -o gsnet_roi-grid.yaml

    The PV-RCNN RoI-grid pooling in place of GSNet's cylinder grouper; nothing else changes.

    grasppanda compose scale_balanced_grasp/sbg_pointnet2-transformer_g1b-realsense_18e \
    --set backbone=ptv3 -o sbg_ptv3.yaml

    Compose wraps PTv3 in FurthestPointSubsample and, because PTv3 returns 64 channels where the grouper needs 256, puts the projected variant of the grouper in place.

    grasppanda compose economicgrasp/economicgrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e \
    --set grouper=multi-scale -o economicgrasp_multi-scale.yaml

    FineGrasp's grouper (crops at four radii, fused by attention) under EconomicGrasp.

    grasppanda compose finegrasp/finegrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e \
    --set head=swad -o finegrasp_swad.yaml

    GSNet's head reads dense labels and FineGrasp trains on compact ones: compose rewrites the training data and the assigner to serve the dense labels.

    grasppanda compose hggd/hggd_r34w8_g1b-realsense_15e \
    --set image_encoder=resnet34 -o hggd_resnet34.yaml

    HGGD has its own stage slot, the image encoder; here a timm ResNet-34 with ImageNet weights.

    Each example was probed and recorded as runs under its method; a unit test holds these examples to the records. A refused combination writes nothing, and every refusal says in brackets why: incompatible by design (structural), not supported yet (not-implemented), needs a setting compose adds (fixed-by-compose), needs a preset's settings (not-a-combination) or needs your consent (policy). The rules are on Swap rules.

    What compose prints (the Scale-Balanced-Grasp example) 
    grasppanda compose scale_balanced_grasp/sbg_pointnet2-transformer_g1b-realsense_18e --set backbone=ptv3

what compose did
  • backbone: preset `ptv3` (PTv3). Point Transformer V3 (encoder + decoder, Pointcept PT-v3m1) on a
    5 mm grid.
  • wrapped PTv3 in the adapter FurthestPointSubsample (preset `furthest-point-subsample`):
    SubsampledSeeds requires 'subsampled' scene features, but PTv3 returns 'dense' ones (one feature
    per scene point). An adapter changes the model; its notes below say how.
  • FurthestPointSubsample.npoint: 1024, the number of seeds of the proposal
    (`model.proposal.num_seeds`): the points the adapter keeps are the seeds.
  • FurthestPointSubsample: fps_skip_near_origin: true, fps_tie_break: pointnet2_cuda, the sampling
    rules of the encoder it replaces (PointNet2Transformer), so that the adapter keeps the very
    points that encoder ends on.
  • grouper: DepthCylinderProjectionGroup in place of DepthCylinderGroup. DepthCylinderGroup as
    configured adds the seed features to its 256-channel crop features, but FurthestPointSubsample
    returns 64-channel features. DepthCylinderProjectionGroup is the same crop with a linear layer
    in front of its seed residual; it changes the model (the base method has none). `--no-adapter`
    leaves the pair refused.

    The file is a child config of the method config. Its header repeats the command, what compose did and the re-tuning notes of every preset; its body is what you would have written by hand:

    _base_:
  - /…/configs/scale_balanced_grasp/sbg_pointnet2-transformer_g1b-realsense_18e.yaml

model:
  backbone:
    type: FurthestPointSubsample  # `dense` -> `subsampled`, written down
    npoint: 1024                  # = model.proposal.num_seeds
    fps_skip_near_origin: true    # the rule of the encoder it replaces: same seeds
    fps_tie_break: pointnet2_cuda   # the rule of the encoder it replaces: same seeds
    backbone:
      type: PTv3                  # `dense`: one feature per input point
      in_channels: 3              # constant ones; geometry enters through the grid
      grid_size: 0.005
  grouper:
    type: DepthCylinderProjectionGroup   # the same crop with a linear layer in front of its seed residual
    radius: 0.08
    ...

    Outside the shipped config tree the base is named by its absolute path: the file can be moved on this machine, and on another one the command in its header composes it again.

    What compose does for you, each time saying so

    No rule about what fits what lives in compose: every verdict is the contract rules of the config checker, quoted. Where exactly one registered piece bridges a refused pair, compose puts it in and says so; --no-adapter leaves the pair refused.

    • An encoder adapter: FeaturePropagation around a sub-sampling encoder under a proposal that scores every point, FurthestPointSubsample around a dense encoder under a proposal that takes the encoder's subsample as its seeds. The adapter gets the seed count of the proposal and the sampling rules of the encoder it replaces.
    • A fitted width: where the new encoder has an out_channels option it is set to the seed width the grouper fixes, otherwise to the width of the encoder it replaces. Where the encoder has no such option, the projected variant of the grouper (DepthCylinderProjectionGroup) takes its place. Where the method feeds real point features (FineGrasp's normals), in_channels is set to their number.
    • An axis adapter between a grouper and a head bundle whose feature axes differ: DepthEmbedGroup (preset depth-embed) or DepthPoolGroup (preset depth-pool).
    • The options a stage declares for the other stages: assigner options that return a field a stage reads, graspness only for a scoring proposal next to ObjectnessViewStage, the decode option of a head that would multiply in a score no stage predicts.
    • The label set of the head bundle: a head of dense label grids on a recipe of compact labels, or the reverse, gets the dataset section and the assigner of a shipped recipe that serves them (--set labels=dense|compact asks for it explicitly). Compact labels are an asset to build (grasppanda prepare <dataset> --compact-labels --config <the composed config>).
    • The dataset section a stage reads, when its preset carries one (tolerance, scale_statistic, graspness). The asset still has to be built; the preset says how.
    • Replace, never merge: a --set of the base's own component type gets _delete_: true. --unset key.path writes a component default back. -o refuses an existing file without --force and never writes onto the base config.

    A composed file can be the base of a new command: compose reads the request in its header and composes again from its base with both requests merged (a config chain is two levels deep). Further overrides go through --opts; a path below a slot you set addresses that component wherever an adapter put it:

    grasppanda compose scale_balanced_grasp/sbg_pointnet2-transformer_g1b-realsense_18e \
    --set backbone=ptv3 --opts model.backbone.grid_size=0.01 -o sbg_ptv3_1cm.yaml
    Two refusals, and what they say

    Stages that do not fit each other. A head bundle supervised with compact labels, fed by an assigner of dense ones that you set yourself:

    $ grasppanda compose economicgrasp/economicgrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e \
      --set assigner=NearestLabelAssigner
...
  ✗ assigner · head [structural]: NearestLabelAssigner returns 'dense' matched labels, but
    EconomicHead is supervised with 'compact' ones. A head bundle and the assigner that feeds it
    must agree on the label set; the dataset must serve that label set too.
...
✗ refused: nothing was written. The messages above name the conflict and a fix.
  → EconomicHead reads compact labels and NearestLabelAssigner returns dense ones; the request set
    the assigner (`--set assigner=...`). Without it compose gives EconomicHead an assigner and
    training data of compact labels; or keep NearestLabelAssigner with a head bundle that reads
    dense labels (DecoupledGridHead, OperationHead, SWADHead).

    Leave the assigner to compose and swap the head instead: compose then rewrites the recipe, as in the FineGrasp example above.

    What compose would insert, refused without it. With --no-adapter, or in a config written by hand:

    $ grasppanda compose graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e \
      --set grouper=ball --no-adapter
...
  ✗ grouper · head [fixed-by-compose]: LocalGroup emits features with axes ('seed', 'channel'), but
    OperationHead expects ('seed', 'depth', 'channel'). An axis is never added or squeezed
    implicitly: wrap the grouper in the axis adapter DepthEmbedGroup (preset `depth-embed`), `{type:
    DepthEmbedGroup, num_depth: <the head's num_depth>, grouper: {...}}`, which `grasppanda compose`
    inserts, or use a grouper and a head bundle that agree.

    A slot the method does not have is refused with the slots it has:

    $ grasppanda compose hggd/hggd_r34w8_g1b-realsense_15e --set backbone=ptv3
✗ HGGD has no slot 'backbone'. Its slots: image_encoder. HGGD is a detector family of its own: the
  stages of the seed-based methods (backbone, proposal, view, grouper, head) do not exist in it.

    3. Probe it¶

    grasppanda probe my.yaml

    A probe is a miniature of grasppanda train: the real trainer with the config's optimizer, schedule and hooks, 20 iterations on the eight frames of the split sample of the config's dataset (GraspNet-1Billion: scene 0, frames 0, 32, …, 224), then prediction on two of them. Nothing is written. The line probe of the report lists the overrides that turn the config into that miniature.

    What each line of the report says
    Line What it tells you
    losses Every loss term at the first and the last iteration, then its fall: from its first active value (reported and not exactly 0) to the mean of the last quarter of its active iterations, and its flags: flat (moved less than 5 %), rising (the last half of the active iterations more than 10 % above the first half), zero at end (0 or missing at the last iteration), constant (the very same value every time). Falling on eight frames means the stages and the labels fit together, nothing more; --json has every iteration's value.
    seeds, real The fraction of seed slots that held a real seed. Below 1 the proposal let fewer points through than it has seeds; at 0 an iteration trained the proposal only.
    seeds, supervised The fraction of seeds that took part in the grasp losses: about half for GraspNet-baseline (seeds on an object), 1 for GSNet by construction, about a tenth for EconomicGrasp with an untrained proposal (a seed counts only within 5 mm of a label point).
    predicted Grasps per frame. A model trained for twenty iterations often predicts few, and the count changes from run to run.
    scores The smallest, the median and the largest score of the grasps of each predicted frame, in the detector's own score unit.
    gradient The units of the model that never received a gradient before an optimizer step: sub-modules at depth 2 and parameters a top-level part holds itself. A top-level part none of whose units was reached is a dead stage and makes the run runs-unstable.
    changed The top-level parts whose trainable weights did not change between the first and the last optimizer step. A frozen encoder is expected here; a stage that should learn is not.
    lr The learning rate of the first optimizer at the first and at the last step, and their ratio. With --schedule constant the line is schedule and names the one rate every step used.
    The schedule of a probe, and why a probe does not repeat

    To fit 20 iterations the probe sets train.epochs to as few epochs as the eight frames need, and the recipe's learning-rate schedule runs over those epochs: GraspNet-baseline's first step (epoch 8) is never reached, so its rate stays (the line lr above); GSNet's decay of 0.95 per epoch ends at 0.63 of its rate, EconomicGrasp's cosine at 2.4 %, and FineGrasp's warm-up of 800 iterations keeps the whole probe below 2.5 % of its rate. --schedule constant removes the scheduler and trains every step at the recipe's peak rate, scaled by the ratio of batch sizes when the probe's batches hold fewer frames than the recipe's (FineGrasp's 0.008 for batches of 32 becomes 0.002), and holds a per-epoch batch-norm momentum decay (GraspNet-baseline, Scale-Balanced-Grasp, HGGD) at its first value. The config hash does not change. The default, --schedule recipe, runs the recipe as shipped; the probes behind Does it run? use constant, so that the trends of different methods compare.

    A probe is one run. Run it again and the hash, the parameter count and the first losses are the same; the last losses, the seed fractions and the grasp counts are not. Training on a GPU is not bit-reproducible here, and after the first optimizer step two runs are two trajectories. Read the numbers as "of this run".

    What a probe needs

    The dataset and the label assets of its split sample. They are small; for GraspNet-1Billion, RealSense:

    grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --labels --split sample
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --graspness --camera realsense --split sample
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --compact-labels --split sample \
    --config economicgrasp/economicgrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e     # EconomicGrasp, FineGrasp
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --graspness --camera realsense --split sample \
    --config finegrasp/finegrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e             # FineGrasp
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --tolerance --split sample \
    --config graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --view-grasps --camera realsense --split sample \
    --config hggd/hggd_r34w8_g1b-realsense_15e                         # HGGD

    The width histogram of Scale-Balanced-Grasp is the exception. It is a statistic of a whole training split, and eight frames leave bins of it empty, which its generator refuses. So a config that reads it (scale_statistic in its training dataset: every config composed from Scale-Balanced-Grasp, or with --set assigner=scale-balanced) is probed with the reference's own file. Import it once, through a config that names the file by its SHA-256:

    sha256sum <reference repository>/statistic/scale_distribution.npy    # the <sha256> below
grasppanda compose scale_balanced_grasp/sbg_pointnet2-transformer_g1b-realsense_18e \
    --opts data.train.dataset.scale_statistic.source=legacy_import \
           data.train.dataset.scale_statistic.reference_sha256=<sha256> -o sbg_reference_histogram.yaml
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --scale-statistic --import-legacy \
    --scale-statistic-file <reference repository>/statistic/scale_distribution.npy \
    --config sbg_reference_histogram.yaml

    From then on, compose with those two --opts, or hand them to grasppanda probe <config> --opts .... Training on the full split does not need the import: grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --scale-statistic --config <config> builds the native histogram of that split.

    Without the dataset, or without an asset, the probe says what is missing and how to build it, falls back to two iterations on a synthetic scene with random labels and reports wired, never runs. --synthetic asks for that check directly. --iters, --frames, --predict-frames, --split, --device and --workers change what is run; --json prints the whole report; grasppanda probe --help repeats the verdicts and exit statuses.

    4. Read the verdict¶

    A probe ends with exactly one of six verdicts. What the levels composes, runs, learns and predicts add to them is on How we measure.

    Verdict Meaning Exit status
    runs On real frames: the detector was built, every requested training iteration ran with finite losses and at least one real seed, every top-level part of the model that holds trainable parameters received a gradient in at least one iteration, the weights stayed finite, and prediction returned one valid grasp group for every probed frame, at least one of them with a grasp. 0
    runs-empty As runs, except that no probed frame has a grasp: prediction returned one valid grasp group for every probed frame and every group is empty. Every stage ran; a model trained for a few iterations often keeps no seed. A run that is also unstable is runs-unstable. 0
    runs-unstable On real frames: the detector was built, trained and predicted, but a loss or a weight became non-finite (a non-finite loss ends the training there), or an iteration had no real seed (no scene point passed the proposal, so only the proposal was trained), or no iteration supervised a seed, or a top-level part with trainable parameters received no gradient in any iteration, or fewer iterations ran or fewer frames were predicted than were asked for. 1
    fails An exception was raised after the config check had passed. The report names the phase (build, data, optimizer, loss, backward, step, hook, predict), the component it came from and the message. 1
    refused The config check refused the config (the same check as grasppanda print-config --check --for train). Nothing was built; the report carries the messages. 2
    wired No real frame was read. On a synthetic table-top scene with random labels the detector was built, back-propagated a loss through every stage and predicted. This shows that the stages connect; it says nothing about real data. 0 with --synthetic, else 3

    runs and runs-empty mean the combination works in this sense: every stage ran. The lines losses, gradient and changed tell a stage that learns from one that is only wired in, in every verdict.

    Reading runs-unstable, runs-empty and fails

    runs-unstable is what an untuned recipe looks like. A deep encoder under a learning rate chosen for a shallow one can lose every seed for a few iterations and recover; the verdict says which iterations. Because a probe is one run, a combination near that edge can be runs in one probe and runs-unstable in the next. Treat the two as one answer, "it works", and watch metric/real_seeds when you train.

    runs-empty is common for a method that keeps only grasps above a score threshold: after twenty iterations no seed may pass it yet. Train longer before you read anything into it.

    fails is a combination that passed the config check and then broke, and the probe says where. The check compares what components and datasets declare; it cannot know whether options suit your data. An encoder named by its type is built with its own defaults:

    $ grasppanda compose gsnet/gsnet_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e --set backbone=OACNNs -o gsnet_oacnns_bare.yaml
...
  • backbone: OACNNs with its constructor defaults; no preset was named. ...
$ grasppanda probe gsnet_oacnns_bare.yaml
...
✗ fails: at loss (iteration 1), in the backbone OACNNs: ContractError: OACNNs: cloud 0 of the batch has no voxel left after 4 of 4 down-sampling steps. ...

    The default grid of OA-CNNs is 2 cm, for rooms; its preset (--set backbone=oacnns) sets 5 mm. A combination that fails for a reason the contracts could have known is a bug of the contract rules: please report it. A probe that ends with CUDA out of memory is fails too, with a note: on a shared machine that says something about the device at that moment, not about the combination.

    5. Train it¶

    grasppanda train my.yaml

    The composed config is an ordinary config: Train and test applies. It is not a tuned recipe: optimizer, schedule, batch size and point budget are the base method's. The header of the composed file lists what to re-tune first. Report a number from such a run as trained here, next to the method as shipped under the same recipe.

    Where the measured answers are¶

    • Explorer: every option under every method, with its verdict and the commands.
    • Does it run?: these steps for every option under each method it fits; refusals and options without a record are named with the reason.
    • Does it train like the shipped component?: the options that run, trained longer beside the component the method ships.
    • configs/experimental/: composed configs kept in the repository, each with the command in its header.
    Adding a preset 
    # One sentence that says what this is.
#
# Free text: where it comes from, what it is not.
#
# What must be re-tuned, and why:
# * option: value, and the reason it will have to change
model:
  backbone:
    type: MyEncoder               # a comment that travels with the line
    grid_size: 0.005

    Save it as grasppanda/presets/<slot>/<name>.yaml. A registered scene encoder without a preset, a stage without a preset or a stated reason, and a component without measured cells each fail a unit test that says what to run (tests/unit/test_combine_presets.py, tests/unit/test_combine_grid.py). How to write the component itself: Add a component.
