    Try a combination¶

    "GSNet with this encoder and that grouper" is four commands:

    grasppanda components --slot backbones           # 1. what exists, with its contract
grasppanda compose <config> --set backbone=...   # 2. a checked config for the combination
grasppanda probe <composed config>               # 3. does it really train and predict?
grasppanda train <composed config>               # 4. the experiment itself

    Steps 2 and 3 take about a minute together. They tell you whether a combination works: it passes the contracts, builds, trains on real frames with finite losses and predicts. They do not tell you how good it is. No AP is attached to anything on this page; that takes step 4 and an evaluation on a benchmark split.

    Which component was found to work under which method is on the generated page Measured combinations; what a swap changes besides the line in the config is in Swapping components. What each option of each slot is, with a command per option, is in Slots.

    The same in a browser: the combination explorer answers a selection of method and options from the measured records and prints the commands, and grasppanda explore serves it locally, with every selection checked by the compose of your installation (nothing is trained). Started with weights (grasppanda explore --weights-dir <dir>), its tab Run a method also runs a ported method, or your selection when the weights fit it, on one frame and shows the grasps: Run in the browser.

    1. See what exists¶

    grasppanda components is the catalogue (Components): every registered component with the contract the config checker compares. grasppanda compose --list shows the presets:

    $ grasppanda compose --list grouper
grouper  --set grouper=<preset or component>
preset                      component                     what it is
──────────────────────────  ────────────────────────────  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
ball                        LocalGroup                    LocalGroup with its defaults: a PointNet++ ball among the seeds, shared MLP and max.
box                         LocalGroup                    LocalGroup over a gripper-aligned box with cyclic padding: PointRCNN's RoI point pooling.
...                                                       (25 presets; shortened here)
voxel-set-abstraction       VoxelSetAbstractionGroup      VoxelSetAbstractionGroup with its defaults: PV-RCNN's keypoint feature for every seed.
  components without a preset: CylinderGroup, CylinderInteractionGroup

A component can also be named by its type (`--set grouper=CylinderGroup`): it is then
built with its constructor defaults, which `grasppanda components <type>` lists.
One preset with its notes: grasppanda compose --show <slot>=<preset>.

    grasppanda components <name> ends with the presets of that component, and both commands take the slot by either name (grouper, groupers).

    The options that fit one method¶

    Name a method config before --list and compose lists only the options of that method's slots, each with what was measured for it: the verdict of its probe, the four levels composes, runs, learns and predicts under that method, its class at each level across the methods it applies to, the grasps per frame and the fall of the loss terms; the options that do not fit with the reason, its kind and the lift where the record has them; the options without a record as not probed, with the reason when there is one:

    Without a slot it lists every slot of the method; --json prints everything, every refusal message whole. The same lists are a page per method (GSNet, HGGD, ...) and the lanes of a method in the explorer, so the three always offer the same options. A config composed from a method config is listed as that method, with a note that the options were measured on the method config. The shipped method configs name the command above each slot. In a wheel the records come with the package; in a checkout they are read from docs/advanced_guides/measured_combinations.json.

    A preset is the part of a config a newcomer cannot guess: the settings of one component for table-top depth clouds of GraspNet-1Billion (grid size, radii, widths, sampling rules) and, where its stage reads a label asset, the dataset section that serves it. Its header says what must be re-tuned and why. Every scene encoder has one, because the constructor defaults of most encoders are those of room-scale benchmarks: a bare {type: PTv3} works on a 2 cm grid with six input channels. A stage whose constructor defaults are the reference values of its method needs none.

    grasppanda compose --show backbone=ptv3     # the file, with its notes

    Presets live in grasppanda/presets/<slot>/<name>.yaml. They are config fragments, not configs, and they are not inherited: two files of one _base_ list may not set the same value, and a preset by definition sets what the method's own model already sets. The values of a preset therefore belong in the child config, which is where compose writes them.

    2. Compose¶

      --set backbone=pointnet2 --set grouper=multi-scale -o gsnet_pointnet2_multi-scale.yaml

    requires 'dense' scene features, but PointNet2 returns 'subsampled' ones (features on a
    sub-sampled point set). An adapter changes the model; its notes below say how.

    What compose does beyond copying presets, each time saying so:

    • It inserts an encoder adapter when the contract between encoder and proposal refuses the pair and exactly one registered adapter bridges it: FeaturePropagation around a sub-sampling encoder under a proposal that scores every point (above), FurthestPointSubsample around a dense encoder under a proposal that takes the encoder's subsample as its seeds. The adapter gets the seed count of the proposal and the sampling rules of the encoder it replaces. --no-adapter leaves the pair refused.
    • It fits a new encoder to the method. Where the encoder has an out_channels option it is set to the seed width the grouper of the composed model fixes (DepthCylinderGroup with seed_residual: gated adds seed features to its crops) and otherwise to the width of the encoder it replaces, so the other stages keep the width they have in the reference model. Both widths are what the components declare (Backbone.output_channels, Grouper.required_seed_channels); nothing is built.
    • It puts the projected variant of a grouper in place when the encoder has no such option and returns another width than the grouper fixes: DepthCylinderProjectionGroup, the same crop with a linear layer in front of its seed residual (Grouper, section 5). That changes the model, and compose says so; --no-adapter leaves the pair refused.
    • Where the method feeds real point features (point_features: observation, FineGrasp), in_channels is set to their number.
    • It inserts an axis adapter between a grouper and a head bundle whose feature axes differ: DepthEmbedGroup (preset depth-embed) repeats a (seed, channel) crop over the depth bins of the head with a learned embedding of the bin, DepthPoolGroup (preset depth-pool) takes the maximum of a (seed, depth, channel) crop over the bins. --no-adapter leaves the pair refused.
    • It sets the options a stage declares for the other stages: the assigner options that return a field a stage reads (supervise: object_seeds, view_widths: true for ObjectnessViewStage); graspness only for a scoring proposal next to ObjectnessViewStage, which then owns objectness (ScoredSeeds with select_by: graspness_only; GraspnessFPS keeps the options of the reference, so it is replaced by that ScoredSeeds); and the decode option of a head that would multiply in a score no stage predicts (OperationHead with score_decode: score_x_tolerance). Each changes the model and is one sentence of the report; --no-adapter leaves the pair refused.
    • It writes the label set of the head bundle into the recipe. A head of dense label grids on a recipe of compact labels, or the other way round, gets the dataset section of a shipped recipe that serves them (the compact_labels section, or the SampleGraspLabels step) and the assigner of a shipped config that returns them; an assigner you set yourself is kept. --set labels=dense or --set labels=compact asks for it in so many words. Compact labels are an asset to build (grasppanda prepare <dataset> --compact-labels --config <the composed config>); dense grids are read from the label store of grasppanda prepare <dataset> --labels.
    • It adds the dataset section a stage reads, when the preset carries one and the base config has none (scale_statistic for ScaleBalancedAssigner, tolerance for OperationHead, graspness for GraspnessFPS). The asset still has to be built; the preset says how. A component named by its type brings no section: the config check then refuses the config and compose prints the --opts that adds it and the preset that carries it.
    • It puts an option back to its default with --unset key.path (a child config cannot delete what it inherits, so the default of the component is written): --unset data.train.dataset.compact_labels.
    • It replaces, it does not merge. A --set of the same component type as the base config's gets _delete_: true, so no option of the base leaks into what you asked for.
    • It does not overwrite. -o refuses a file that exists unless you pass --force, and never writes onto the base config.

    The base is a config that inherits fragments only, like the method configs under configs/<method>/: a config chain is two levels deep, and the composed file is the second. So a composed file cannot be inherited from, but it can be the base of a command: compose reads the request in its header and composes again from its base with the two requests merged.

    $ grasppanda compose gsnet_pointnet2_multi-scale.yaml --set head=swad --opts train.epochs=20 -o second.yaml
grasppanda compose gsnet/gsnet_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e --set backbone=pointnet2 --set grouper=multi-scale --set head=swad --opts train.epochs=20
...
what compose did
  • gsnet_pointnet2_multi-scale.yaml is itself a composed config, and a config chain is limited to 2
    levels: this config is composed from its base, gsnet/gsnet_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e, with its
    request and yours merged (a `--set` of yours replaces the one of the same slot).

    A composed file you edited by hand, and a shipped child config such as the cross-dataset …-to-gc6d-d435 ones, are refused as a base with the config to compose from instead.

    Further overrides go through --opts; a path below a slot you set addresses that component wherever an adapter put it:

    grasppanda compose gsnet/gsnet_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e --set backbone=ptv3 \
    --opts model.backbone.grid_size=0.01 train.epochs=20 -o gsnet_ptv3_1cm.yaml

    No rule about what fits what lives in compose. Every verdict is the contract rules of the config checker, quoted, and a refused combination writes nothing. Each refusal says in brackets why it is one (Swapping, Why a pair is refused): structural (the components cannot work together), not-implemented (a lift not written yet, named in the message), fixed-by-compose (what compose inserts by itself, met with --no-adapter or in a config written by hand), not-a-combination (a component without a required option, two options that disagree) or policy. The --json report lists the same in refusal_kinds, in the order of refusal.

    A probe is a miniature of grasppanda train, not a loop of its own: the real trainer, the config's optimizer, schedule, accumulation, clipping and hooks, on the eight frames of the split sample (scene 0 of GraspNet-1Billion, frames 0, 32, …, 224), then prediction on two of them. Nothing is written. The line probe lists the overrides that turn the config into that miniature; with them, grasppanda train runs the same thing.

    The schedule of a probe. To fit 20 iterations the probe sets train.epochs to as few epochs as the eight frames need, and the recipe's learning-rate schedule runs over those epochs: GSNet's decay of 0.95 per epoch ends at 0.63 of its rate, EconomicGrasp's cosine at 2.4 %, and FineGrasp's warm-up of 800 iterations keeps the whole probe below 2.5 % of its rate. The trends of two methods are then not comparable. --schedule constant removes the scheduler (scheduler=null among the overrides) and trains every step at the recipe's peak rate: the rate of the optimizer (with the per-group multipliers of ParamGroups), or max_lr for OneCycleLR and CyclicLR. When the probe's batches hold fewer frames than the recipe's, that peak is scaled by their ratio (the linear scaling rule; FineGrasp's 0.008 for batches of 32 becomes 0.002 on the 8 frames of a grid probe), and a note says so. A batch-norm momentum that the recipe decays per epoch (BNMomentumHook of GraspNet-baseline, Scale-Balanced-Grasp and HGGD) is held at its first epoch's value the same way (hooks.<i>.decay=1 among the overrides, and a note): decayed over epochs of a few iterations it reaches its floor of 0.001 within the probe, and the running statistics that prediction normalises with stop following the weights (Scale-Balanced-Grasp then predicted no grasp after 250 iterations). The optimizer with its weight decay, the clipping, the accumulation and every other hook stay the recipe's, and the config hash does not change. In place of the line lr the probe prints schedule constant: 0.001, the recipe's peak rate, at every step. The default, --schedule recipe, runs the recipe as shipped; the measured grid probes with constant.

    A probe is one run, and it does not repeat. Run the command again and the hash, the parameter count and the first column of losses are the same (the first losses to about six digits); the last column, the seed fractions and the grasp counts are not. Training on a GPU is not bit-reproducible here, and after the first optimizer step two runs are two trajectories. Two probes of the shipped GSNet config on one machine: total loss 17.43 at the first iteration both times, 1.89 and 1.72 at the last; 233 and 279 grasps on the two frames, then 164 and 188; the smallest fraction of real seeds 0.65, then 0.68. The probe shown above predicted 1024 grasps on both frames in the run just before it. So read the numbers as "of this run", and a verdict on the edge the same way: see The verdict.

    Line What it tells you
    losses Every loss term at the first and the last iteration, then its fall: from its first active value (reported and not exactly 0) to the mean of the last quarter of its active iterations, and its flags: flat (moved less than 5 %), rising (the last half of the active iterations more than 10 % above the first half), zero at end (0 or missing at the last iteration), constant (the very same value every time). A grasp term that is 0 until a seed is supervised starts where it becomes active, and the line says in how many iterations it was. Falling on eight frames means the stages and the labels fit together, nothing more; --json has every iteration's value.
    seeds, real The fraction of seed slots that held a real seed. Below 1 the proposal let fewer points through than it has seeds; at 0 an iteration trained the proposal only.
    seeds, supervised The fraction of seeds that took part in the grasp losses: 1 for GSNet by construction, about a tenth for EconomicGrasp with an untrained proposal (a seed counts only within 5 mm of a label point), about half for GraspNet-baseline (seeds on an object).
    predicted Grasps per frame. A model trained for twenty iterations often predicts few, and the count changes from run to run. When no frame has a grasp the verdict is runs-empty: every stage ran, and the detector dropped the grasps of seeds that are not valid (no point passed the proposal, or the view stage classified the seed as background) or below its score threshold, at the very end.
    scores The smallest, the median and the largest score of the grasps of each predicted frame, in the detector's own score unit.
    gradient The units of the model that never received a gradient (an L2 norm above 1e-8) before an optimizer step: sub-modules at depth 2, such as backbone.encoder or head.score_net, and parameters a top-level part holds itself. A top-level part none of whose units was reached is named alone and makes the run runs-unstable (a dead stage); a unit inside a part that learned is reported, not judged.
    changed The top-level parts whose trainable weights did not change between before the first optimizer step and after the last one (a relative change of at most 1e-6). A frozen encoder is expected here; a stage that should learn is not.
    lr The learning rate of the first optimizer at the first and at the last step, and their ratio. A schedule that steps per epoch decays with the epochs of the probe, which follow from the batch size and the eight frames: under GSNet's recipe, 0.95 per epoch at 2 iterations per epoch, the last of 20 steps runs at 0.95⁹ = 0.63 of the first rate. With --schedule constant the line is schedule and names the one rate every step used.
    trained, memory Seconds per iteration and peak GPU memory of this process, on this machine at this moment.

    The verdict¶

    A probe ends with exactly one of six verdicts.

    Verdict Meaning Exit status
    runs On real frames: the detector was built, every requested training iteration ran with finite losses and at least one real seed, every top-level part of the model that holds trainable parameters received a gradient in at least one iteration, the weights stayed finite, and prediction returned one valid grasp group for every probed frame, at least one of them with a grasp. 0
    runs-empty As runs, except that no probed frame has a grasp: prediction returned one valid grasp group for every probed frame and every group is empty. Every stage ran; a model trained for a few iterations often keeps no seed. A run that is also unstable is runs-unstable. 0
    runs-unstable On real frames: the detector was built, trained and predicted, but a loss or a weight became non-finite (a non-finite loss ends the training there), or an iteration had no real seed (no scene point passed the proposal, so only the proposal was trained), or no iteration supervised a seed, or a top-level part with trainable parameters received no gradient in any iteration, or fewer iterations ran or fewer frames were predicted than were asked for. 1
    fails An exception was raised after the config check had passed. The report names the phase (build, data, optimizer, loss, backward, step, hook, predict), the component it came from and the message. 1
    refused The config check refused the config (the same check as grasppanda print-config --check --for train). Nothing was built; the report carries the messages. 2
    wired No real frame was read. On a synthetic table-top scene with random labels the detector was built, back-propagated a loss through every stage and predicted. This shows that the stages connect; it says nothing about real data. 0 with --synthetic, else 3

    runs-unstable is what an untuned recipe looks like. A deep encoder under a learning rate chosen for a shallow one can lose every seed for a few iterations and recover; the verdict says which iterations, and the combination still works in the sense of this page. Because a probe is one run and the seed fractions differ between runs, a combination near that edge can be runs in one probe and runs-unstable in the next. Treat the two verdicts as one answer, "it works", and the difference as a hint to watch metric/real_seeds when you train.

    runs-empty is common for a method that keeps only grasps above a score threshold: after twenty iterations no seed may pass it yet (GSNet with the PointNeXt encoder predicts no grasp on either frame of its probe). It ran every stage, so it works in the sense of this page too; train longer before you read anything into it. The lines losses, gradient and changed are what tells a stage that learns from one that is only wired in: a flat term, a unit without a gradient or a part that did not change is reported in every verdict and never hidden by a falling total.

    fails is a combination that passed the config check and then broke. The probe says where. The config check compares what components and datasets declare; what it cannot know is whether options suit your data. An encoder named by its type instead of its preset is built with its own defaults, and compose says so:

    $ grasppanda compose gsnet/gsnet_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e --set backbone=OACNNs -o gsnet_oacnns_bare.yaml
...
what compose did
  • backbone: OACNNs with its constructor defaults; no preset was named. Its options are the
    component's own defaults, not a recipe for table-top clouds; that is what its preset holds:
    `--set backbone=oacnns`.
...
$ grasppanda probe gsnet_oacnns_bare.yaml
...
✗ fails: at loss (iteration 1), in the backbone OACNNs: ContractError: OACNNs: cloud 0 of the batch has no voxel left after 4 of 4 down-sampling steps. With crop_odd_extent=True the sparse grid is the bounding box of the cloud and loses the last cell of an odd axis at every level, so a cloud has to span at least 16 voxels along every axis. Use a smaller grid_size, or set crop_odd_extent=False to keep every voxel.

    The default grid of OA-CNNs is 2 cm, for rooms; the preset sets 5 mm. That is what a preset is for, and the probe is what tells you in a minute instead of after a queue.

    A combination that fails for a reason the contracts could have known is a bug of the contract rules: it should be refused before anything is built, or run. Two such families were found by probing and are refused now, by two rules shown in Compose above: a model on a data recipe that does not serve its labels (which compose resolves for the label set of the head), and an encoder of another width under a grouper that fixes one (which compose resolves with the grouper's projected variant). If a probe shows you a third, please report it.

    A probe that ends with CUDA out of memory is fails too, with a note: on a shared machine that is a statement about the device at that moment, not about the combination.

    What a probe needs¶

    The dataset and the label assets of its split sample. They are small; for GraspNet-1Billion, RealSense:

    grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --labels --split sample
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --graspness --camera realsense --split sample
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --compact-labels --split sample \
    --config economicgrasp/economicgrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e     # EconomicGrasp, FineGrasp
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --graspness --camera realsense --split sample \
    --config finegrasp/finegrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e             # FineGrasp
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --tolerance --split sample \
    --config graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --view-grasps --camera realsense --split sample \
    --config hggd/hggd_r34w8_g1b-realsense_15e                         # HGGD

    The width histogram of Scale-Balanced-Grasp is the exception. It is a statistic of a whole training split, and eight frames leave bins of it empty, which its generator refuses. So a config that reads it (scale_statistic in its training dataset: every config composed from Scale-Balanced-Grasp, or with --set assigner=scale-balanced) is probed with the reference's own file, which does not depend on the split. Import it once. The import needs a config that names the file by its SHA-256, which compose writes:

    SHA=$(sha256sum <reference repository>/statistic/scale_distribution.npy | cut -d' ' -f1)
grasppanda compose scale_balanced_grasp/sbg_pointnet2-transformer_g1b-realsense_18e \
    --opts data.train.dataset.scale_statistic.source=legacy_import \
           data.train.dataset.scale_statistic.reference_sha256=$SHA -o sbg_reference_histogram.yaml
grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --scale-statistic --import-legacy \
    --scale-statistic-file <reference repository>/statistic/scale_distribution.npy \
    --config sbg_reference_histogram.yaml

    From then on, either compose with those two --opts, or hand them to the probe:

    grasppanda probe <config> --opts \
    data.train.dataset.scale_statistic.source=legacy_import \
    data.train.dataset.scale_statistic.reference_sha256=$SHA

    Without it the probe of such a config says exactly this, runs the synthetic check and ends wired (exit status 3). Training on the full split does not need the import: grasppanda prepare graspnet1b --scale-statistic --config <config> builds the native histogram of that split (about 3200 frames).

    Without the dataset, or without an asset, the probe says what is missing and how to build it, falls back to a wiring check on a synthetic scene and reports wired, never runs:

    4. Train¶

    grasppanda train gsnet_pointnet2_multi-scale.yaml

    The composed config is an ordinary config: everything in Training and testing applies. What it is not is a tuned recipe. The optimizer, schedule, batch size and point budget are the base method's, chosen by its authors for its own stages; the header of the composed file lists what to re-tune first, and metric/real_seeds and metric/supervised_seeds in the training log are the first numbers to compare with a run of the reference config. A number from such a run has no paper to be compared with: report it as trained here, next to the reference under the same recipe, and call it experimental.

    configs/experimental/ holds eight such configs. They are compose's output for the command in their header and are regenerated with the presets (python -m tools.make_combination_docs).

    Reading the measured page¶

    Measured combinations is steps 2 and 3 run for a grid: every scene encoder under every seed-based method, every other stage with one stage replaced at a time, a sample of the legal sets of stages (each composed onto the method whose data recipe serves its labels), HGGD as it is and with every image-encoder preset, every post-processing preset under all six methods, every recipe preset under every method it applies to, every loss preset in one term of every kind it accepts, and a few requests that replace several slots at once. Grid probes run with --schedule constant, so that the trends of different methods compare. The explorer shows the same records per selection. Every cell is a recorded outcome; its two commands are in the measured file beside the page. Typing them repeats the experiment: the same config (hash for hash), the same parameter count, the same first losses. It does not repeat the later numbers: a cell is one run. In the tables

    • ✓ is runs; ✓ own is the method's own component (the cell composes to the shipped config, hash for hash); ✓ in Adapter ran after compose inserted that adapter; † means compose also set out_channels or in_channels to fit the method;
    • ∅ is runs-empty (it ran and predicted no grasp on either frame), ≈ (n) is runs-unstable, ✗ (n) is refused (the config check's verdict: nothing was built) and fails (n) passed the config check and broke; n points to the reason below the table;
    • below the mark of a probe: the grasps it predicted per frame and, for a record of judge 2, the range of the fall of its loss terms and the levels it did not reach; a probe that predicted no grasp on any frame is greyed;
    • the last column is the class of the component, level by level. A cell is decided at four levels from its record: composes (the config check accepts it), runs (the probe ran clean: runs or runs-empty; runs-unstable counts as not running only when a repeat of the probe was unstable too, and stays unstable, not repeated until then), learns (every loss term active in at least half of the iterations fell by at least 5 %, none flagged flat, rising or constant, every unit reached by a gradient, every part changed) and predicts (grasps on every predicted frame, scores not all the same). The last two need judge 2: a record of the first judge says not checked. The class counts the methods under which the option reached a level: universal (all five seed-based methods), most (three or four), some (two), specific (exactly one), none; an option probed under fewer methods, or with undecided cells, reads N of M probed with the rest named after it (+1 not checked). A refusal by policy (remote code) waits for your consent and is never counted as none;
    • the columns Fall per loss term and Learns, predicts of the numbers below a table say what judge 2 saw in a run and what kept it from a level; a record made before that judge says not checked. The sections Judges and Measured classes of the page define every field and every threshold, from grasppanda.combine.levels.

    The tables Measured classes near the top of the page list every component under its class, one table per level: that is the short answer to "what works where". Every class is "one run, seed 0, 20 iterations, 8 frames of one scene": it counts verdicts, not accuracy.

    A class is a statement about that grid: those presets, the methods' own recipes, one stage replaced at a time. specific for a head bundle means that it cannot be swapped in alone, because it needs its assigner and its labels; the table Sets of stages shows combinations in which it runs (the sets are too many to probe each: the page says how many were, and how they were picked), and Several slots at once a few requests that also replace the encoder, a loss or the recipe. A set that reads dense label grids is refused under EconomicGrasp and FineGrasp, whose recipes serve compact labels, and the reverse; the page does not hold those cells, the config check says it when you compose one. And a class says nothing about accuracy: universal at runs means "builds, trains and predicts under every method", not "is a good idea under every method".

    Whether an option that runs also learns like the stage it replaces is the next level: Learns like the reference? trains the cells that run for 250 iterations and compares every loss term with the method as shipped under the same settings (on a par, worse, diverging). One run per cell, eight frames of one scene: a statement about training dynamics, still not about accuracy.

    The section Passed the check, failed afterwards lists the gaps this grid met: combinations the config check accepted and a component then refused to build or run. The toolbox treats each as a bug of its contract rules. An empty list says that no cell of the grid is such a case; it is not a proof about configs outside the grid.

    Adding a preset¶

    # One sentence that says what this is.
#
# Free text: where it comes from, what it is not.
#
# What must be re-tuned, and why:
# * option: value, and the reason it will have to change
model:
  backbone:
    type: MyEncoder               # a comment that travels with the line
    grid_size: 0.005

    Save it as grasppanda/presets/<slot>/<name>.yaml. A registered scene encoder without a preset, a stage without a preset or a stated reason, and a component without measured cells each fail a unit test that says what to run (tests/unit/test_combine_presets.py, tests/unit/test_combine_grid.py).
