    Inference and visualisation¶

    Four tools take a trained detector off the benchmark:

    Tool What it does
    grasppanda demo grasps on one frame, as numbers, a picture and an animation
    grasppanda explore the same in a browser page: pick a method and a weights file, run it on the example scene or on your depth image
    GraspInferencer the same from Python: a depth image or a point cloud in, a GraspGroup out
    grasppanda analyze says where the AP of an evaluation comes from, and how two evaluations differ

    All command output on this page was produced by the commands shown, on the development machine (one RTX A6000, shared). Paths are as printed.

    You bring the weights

    The model zoo has no weights to download yet: the one model trained with GraspPanda so far (GSNet) is not published, and the checkpoints the authors of the methods released are under their own, mostly non-commercial, licences. No grasp checkpoint is downloaded for you (only an image-encoder preset with pretrained: true fetches backbone weights, through its library, and says so). You download an author checkpoint yourself, convert it once with tools/convert_checkpoint.py, and pass the result with --weights. Each method's page walks through it in five minutes, with the file, its licence, the commands and what they printed when they were measured: GSNet, EconomicGrasp, FineGrasp, GraspNet-baseline, Scale-Balanced-Grasp, HGGD.

    First grasps in five minutes¶

    Without downloading anything¶

    The example project trains a toy detector on a CPU in about a second. It predicts nothing useful, but it goes through every step a real method goes through, so it tells you that your installation works:

    grasppanda train projects/example_project/configs/toy_train.yaml --opts runtime.run_id=toy
grasppanda demo  projects/example_project/configs/toy_train.yaml \
    --weights work_dirs/toy_train/toy/checkpoints/best.pth --example

    --example is a synthetic table-top scene that ships with the package: six geometric primitives on a table, ray-cast into a 1280 x 720 depth image in millimetres, a colour image, the camera matrix and an object mask. It is computed from formulas, so it needs no dataset and carries no dataset licence. It is not a camera image: surfaces are perfectly smooth and there is no noise, no missing depth and no reflection.

    With a real method¶

    Take GraspNet-baseline: it needs no sparse-convolution library and runs on a CPU. Get checkpoint-rs.tar from the link in its page (non-commercial research licence), then:

    python -m tools.convert_checkpoint checkpoint-rs.tar baseline_realsense.pth \
    --source-format graspnet-baseline --camera realsense
grasppanda demo graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e \
    --weights baseline_realsense.pth --example --collision-filter

    What the lines say:

    • pipeline is the test pipeline of the config, step by step. The demo runs exactly these steps on the input; it has no preprocessing of its own.
    • cloud: the workspace crop left 316,254 points and the model was given 20,000 of them.
    • grasps: how many the model emitted, and how many the collision filter kept. The time is that of the first call, which includes loading GPU kernels; later calls are faster. On the CPU of the same machine (--device cpu) this call took 1.0 s.

    What the result does not say: how good the method is. The scene is synthetic and outside the training distribution of every model here; whether a grasp would hold is not checked. For numbers, see Training and testing and Reading an analysis.

    The five methods¶

    Every shipped method runs through the same command. What differs is what each reads and how its authors' checkpoint is converted:

    Method Config Reads --source-format Needs
    GraspNet-baseline graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e points graspnet-baseline nothing extra
    Scale-Balanced-Grasp scale_balanced_grasp/sbg_pointnet2-transformer_g1b-realsense_18e points scale-balanced-grasp nothing extra
    HGGD hggd/hggd_r34w8_g1b-realsense_15e (or -kinect_) colour and depth images, intrinsics, points hggd a colour image
    GSNet gsnet/gsnet_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e points graspness-unofficial an spconv extra
    EconomicGrasp economicgrasp/economicgrasp_sunet14_g1b-realsense_10e (or -kinect_) points economicgrasp an spconv extra

    All five were run with --example --collision-filter and on a GraspNet-1Billion frame while this page was written. Weights for a RealSense config were trained on RealSense D435 images, weights for a Kinect config on Azure Kinect images.

    Your own camera¶

    What you need¶

    • A depth image: single-channel, 16-bit PNG or TIFF (or a .npy). An integer image is read as millimetres, a float image as metres; --depth-scale N says otherwise (N depth units per metre).
    • The intrinsics of the depth camera in pixels: --intrinsics fx,fy,cx,cy, or a file holding those four numbers or the 3 x 3 camera matrix. They must be the intrinsics of the resolution the image has.
    • For HGGD, the colour image registered to the depth image, pixel for pixel (--rgb). Point-cloud methods ignore it, except to colour the picture.
    grasppanda demo graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e \
    --weights baseline_realsense.pth --depth camera/depth.png --intrinsics 925,925,640,360 \
    --workspace -0.25 -0.2 0.35 0.25 0.2 0.8 --collision-filter --out work_dirs/camera_baseline

      input     camera/depth.png: 1280 x 720 uint16, read as millimetres
  cloud     20,000 points to the model, sampled from 445,990 in the workspace
  grasps    942 predicted in 3.51 s (first call, includes warm-up); 240 left after ModelFreeCollisionFilter

    (The files here are the example scene, written to disk by --example. They stand in for a camera so that the command can be shown with its output.)

    The workspace matters¶

    The point-cloud methods were trained and tested on clouds cropped to the objects: the GraspNet-1Billion pipelines crop to a box around the ground-truth segmentation, at test time too (which is why their results are stamped gt_assisted: [workspace]). Your camera has no ground truth. The inferencer does not pretend otherwise; it does what the input allows and says what it did:

    You pass What happens
    --workspace XMIN YMIN ZMIN XMAX YMAX ZMAX only points inside this box of the camera frame are kept (metres; x right, y down, z forward)
    --mask FILE (object pixels non-zero, from any segmentation) and --table-from-camera FILE the config's own provider runs, with your mask in the place of the dataset's segmentation
    --mask FILE alone the same kind of crop with the box aligned to the camera axes instead of the table; a note says so
    nothing every pixel with depth is used, and a note says what that means

    The note of the last case, as printed:

      note      The test pipeline of this config crops the cloud with SegmentationBoxWorkspace, which reads a ground-truth segmentation; the weights were trained and tested on clouds cropped that way. No mask was given, so every pixel with depth is used. Expect grasps on the surroundings too and fewer points on the objects; pass mask=... (object pixels) or workspace=... (a box in the camera frame) to restrict the input.
  cloud     20,000 points to the model, sampled from 699,022 in the workspace

    HGGD is the exception: its pipeline has no workspace (NoWorkspace), it reads the whole image.

    grasppanda demo hggd/hggd_r34w8_g1b-realsense_15e --weights hggd_realsense.pth \
    --depth camera/depth.png --rgb camera/rgb.png --intrinsics 925,925,640,360 --collision-filter

      model     HGGD on cuda, reads depth, intrinsics, points, rgb
  pipeline  data.test: DepthToCloud() -> NoWorkspace() -> RandomSample(num_points=25600)
  variants  raw (no post-processing), cd (ModelFreeCollisionFilter + GraspNMS)
  input     camera/depth.png: 1280 x 720 uint16, read as millimetres; colour camera/rgb.png
  cloud     25,600 points to the model
  grasps    470 predicted in 4.09 s (first call, includes warm-up); 72 left after ModelFreeCollisionFilter + GraspNMS

    The usual mistakes¶

    Each ends with exit code 1 and one line that says what to change. Verbatim:

    $ grasppanda demo ... --depth camera/depth.png
✗ InputError: --depth needs --intrinsics fx,fy,cx,cy: the focal lengths and the principal point of the depth camera in pixels. Without them a depth image cannot become a point cloud. Your camera's SDK reports them (RealSense: rs-enumerate-devices -c).

$ grasppanda demo ... --depth camera/depth.png --intrinsics 925,925,640,360 --depth-scale 1
✗ InputError: the depth image (median value 545, depth_scale=1) has a median range of 545 m. The values are probably millimetres: pass depth_scale=1000 (--depth-scale 1000).

$ grasppanda demo ... --depth camera/depth.png --intrinsics 925,925,640,360 --workspace -0.2 -0.2 1.5 0.2 0.2 2.0
✗ InputError: the workspace is empty: no pixel with depth lies inside it (CameraBoxWorkspace). The cloud spans x -0.45 to 0.45, y -0.19 to 0.16, z 0.42 to 0.84 metres in the camera frame (x right, y down, z forward).

$ grasppanda demo hggd/hggd_r34w8_g1b-realsense_15e --weights hggd_realsense.pth --depth camera/depth.png --intrinsics 925,925,640,360
✗ InputError: HGGD reads the colour image; pass the colour image registered to the depth image: rgb=... (--rgb FILE)

    Intrinsics of another resolution, a colour image of another size than the depth image, a colour-mapped picture of depth instead of a depth image, and a cloud in millimetres are refused in the same way.

    Point clouds and dataset frames¶

    --points cloud.npy|.ply|.pcd takes a cloud in the camera frame, in metres, that you have already cropped to the workspace. The image-space steps of the pipeline do not apply to it; sampling and input features do. A method that reads images refuses it and lists what it needs instead.

    --frame graspnet1b:scene_0100/realsense/0000 reads a frame of the dataset the config names, through the config's own dataset reader:

      input     dataset frame graspnet1b:scene_0100/realsense/0000, read by the config's dataset reader
  cloud     15,000 points to the model, sampled from 519,612 in the workspace; ground-truth-assisted inputs: workspace
  grasps    1024 predicted in 2.58 s (first call, includes warm-up); 816 left after ModelFreeCollisionFilter

    The grasps are those grasppanda test dumps for that frame. This is tested, not assumed (tests/integration/test_inference_api.py): for GraspNet-baseline, Scale-Balanced-Grasp, GSNet and EconomicGrasp the arrays are bit-identical to the engine's dump on all eight frames compared, raw and collision-filtered, and the same holds for GSNet on GraspClutter6D frames. HGGD draws random numbers while it predicts (it jitters its regions). A test run seeds once and draws frame after frame; a call here seeds per call. HGGD therefore reproduces the first frame of a run (within 1e-5; two runs of the same GPU kernels differ by 2e-7) and gives another, equally valid, draw for later frames.

    Run in the browser¶

    grasppanda explore serves the combination explorer on your machine. Give it weights and its tab Run a method runs a ported method in the page, with no code:

    grasppanda explore --weights-dir ~/grasppanda_weights   # every .pth, .pt, .tar, .ckpt under it
grasppanda explore --weights gsnet=work_dirs/gsnet/<run>/checkpoints/epoch_0010.pth \
    --weights hggd=hggd_realsense.pth                   # or files by name (repeatable)

    The panel has four steps and a button:

    1. Method: one of the six ported methods. When you have changed slots in Build a combination, the panel also offers with your changes (or press Run this selection on a scene there): the composed config runs, so the weights must fit the composed model. A change of post-processing or of the training recipe keeps the model; a new stage needs weights trained with it.
    2. Weights: the files the server was started with. A file whose name suggests the method is chosen for you (gsnet, hggd_author_realsense.pth); the run says whether it fits.
    3. Input: the built-in example scene, or your depth image: a 16-bit PNG in millimetres or a .npy in metres, at most 20 MB and 4096 pixels a side, with the intrinsics fx, fy, cx, cy in pixels (pasting 925,925,640,360 into one box fills all four), an optional colour image (HGGD needs it) and an optional workspace box in the camera frame.
    4. Post-processing: the collision filter, the config's cd variant, as --collision-filter applies it.

    Run shows each step while it happens (building the model and loading the weights, reading the input, predicting, post-processing, drawing), then the picture grasppanda demo draws, the best ten grasps, the counts through the post-processing chain, two downloads (every grasp as the (G, 17) array of grasps.npy, and the picture), the lines demo prints under What ran, and the commands that do the same in a terminal. On the development machine (one RTX A6000, shared) the first run after the server started took 7.9 s for GSNet on the example scene, most of it building the model, loading 176 MB of weights and the first CUDA calls; switching to HGGD took 2.3 s, and GraspNet-baseline on the example scene uploaded as depth.png with the workspace box of Your own camera 1.0 s, with the same 942 predicted and 240 kept as the command there. Running the same method again keeps its model in memory.

    When a run cannot go ahead, the panel says why in the words of grasppanda demo: weights that do not fit the config (WeightMismatchError with its list of keys), a missing optional library (MissingDependencyError with the pip install line), an input the inferencer refuses (InputError: no intrinsics, a colour-mapped picture instead of a depth image, an empty workspace, HGGD without a colour image), a GPU without free memory, another run in progress.

    What the server allows, and nothing more:

    • It listens on 127.0.0.1 only, answers requests addressed to a loopback name, and takes a run only from its own page (Host and Origin are checked; a run is JSON, which a page of another site cannot send without the server's consent).
    • It reads weights only from the files its command line named: under --weights-dir (resolved, so .. and links that leave the folder are refused) and each --weights NAME=PATH. The page picks one of those; a path it sends is never opened as it is.
    • An upload is limited to 20 MB, checked against the format its name claims, and decoded into a temporary directory that is removed when the run ends. A run writes nowhere else and downloads nothing (a composed selection is built with pretrained: false, since the weights file supplies every parameter). The only other file the server ever writes is a composed config you ask it to save, into the working directory.
    • One run at a time: a second request is answered with "another run is in progress".
    • A run predicts on one frame. Nothing is trained.

    --device cpu runs on the CPU. On the documentation site the tab is greyed, with the command that enables it, since a run needs your weights on your computer.

    From Python¶

    import numpy as np
from PIL import Image

from grasppanda import GraspInferencer

inferencer = GraspInferencer(
    "graspnet_baseline/graspnet-baseline_pointnet2_g1b-realsense_18e", "baseline_realsense.pth"
)
depth = np.array(Image.open("camera/depth.png"))  # (720, 1280) uint16, millimetres
grasps = inferencer(
    depth=depth,
    intrinsics=(925.0, 925.0, 640.0, 360.0),  # fx, fy, cx, cy in pixels
    depth_scale=1000.0,  # depth units per metre
    workspace=(-0.25, -0.2, 0.35, 0.25, 0.2, 0.8),  # a box in the camera frame, metres
    collision_filter=True,
)
best = grasps.sort_by_score()[0]
print(len(grasps), "grasps in frame", repr(grasps.frame), "score:", grasps.score_kind)
print("grasp point   ", best.translation[0].tolist())
print("approach axis ", best.rotation[0, :, 0].tolist())
print("width, depth  ", float(best.width), float(best.depth))
print("array         ", grasps.to_graspnet_array().shape, grasps.to_graspnet_array().dtype)

    The call takes exactly one kind of input: depth= with intrinsics= (and rgb=, mask=, depth_scale=, table_from_camera=, workspace=), or points=, or frame=. It returns a GraspGroup:

    Field Meaning
    translation (G, 3) the grasp point between the fingers, metres
    rotation (G, 3, 3) column 0 the approach direction, column 1 the closing direction, column 2 the finger-height direction
    width (G,) jaw opening, metres
    depth (G,) how far the fingers reach past the grasp point along the approach, metres
    score (G,) higher is better; score_kind says what it is. It is not a calibrated probability
    frame camera: the optical frame of the depth sensor, x right, y down, z forward

    to_graspnet_array() gives the 17 columns other GraspNet tools read: [score, width, height, depth, R (9, row-major), t (3), object_id]. To move the grasps into your robot's base frame, apply your hand-eye calibration as a 4 x 4 tensor: grasps.transform(base_from_camera, frame="base").

    Things worth knowing:

    • Order. Grasps come in the order the detector emits them. Use sort_by_score().
    • Repeatable. Point sampling and the detector's random draws are seeded per call, from the config (data.<split>.dataset.seed, runtime.seed), so the same input gives the same grasps. seed= changes both.
    • Post-processing. collision_filter=True applies the config's variant that holds a collision filter (cd in the shipped configs); variant="name" applies any chain of evaluation.variants. The filter tests the gripper against the observed points only; it knows nothing about what the camera did not see.
    • Two halves. inferencer.observe(...) returns the Observation the model reads (the sampled cloud, the cloud before sampling, the images), inferencer.predict(observation) the grasps. Use them to look at what the pipeline did to your input.
    • Warnings. Differences from the conditions of the test pipeline (no mask where the pipeline crops with one, a cloud that cannot be checked for a crop) are raised once per inferencer as InferenceWarning.

    What is not claimed¶

    The weights were trained on GraspNet-1Billion: two cameras, 1280 x 720, objects on a table 0.4 to 0.7 m away. Nothing here has been measured on another camera, another resolution or a real robot. HGGD needs an image whose size is a multiple of its 640 x 360 network input, with the same aspect ratio. Treat grasps on your own data as predictions of a model outside its test conditions until you have checked them on your setup.

    Reading an analysis¶

    The header¶

    The first block is everything a number needs before it may be quoted: protocol, split and camera, how many frames were scored, which inputs were derived from ground truth, the post-processing chain, and the weights and code that produced it. extent compares the frames of the record with the split it names. This run scored every 64th frame, so it is a subset, and the report says so in capitals. The weights are the authors' RealSense checkpoint, converted (provenance author-ckpt), and the collision filter ran. Its AP of 0.4867 is a measurement on 120 frames; it is not the AP of GraspNet-baseline on the seen split.

    Where the precision comes from¶

    AP is the mean of a precision table with one column per friction threshold μ. The first table gives the mean of each column; their mean is the AP. Here a grasp that needs μ ≤ 0.2 is rare (0.18), and most of what can be earned is earned by μ ≤ 0.8.

    The second table attributes the AP. A frame's AP is a weighted sum over its 50 ranks: rank 1 owns about 9 % of it, rank 50 owns 0.04 %. Every rank either earns its share, or loses it for exactly one reason: the gripper collides with the scene (checked against the ground-truth models), nothing is between the fingers, the grasp is free but not force-closed at this μ, or the rank was never filled. The columns add up to one. In this run 29 % of the AP is lost to collisions although the model-free collision filter ran, and at μ ≤ 0.8 another 12 % to grasps that are free but do not hold.

    By object applies the same split to the object each ranked grasp belongs to. The numbers are percentage points of AP: object 0 earns 3.07 points and loses 4.99 to collisions, more than any other object. success is the share an object's ranks earned of what they held. Object ids are the dataset's.

    By scene lists the scenes at both ends. Scenes, not frames, are the unit to think in: the four frames of a scene show the same objects.

    Grasps follows the predictions through the post-processing chain. For a chain with two steps each step has its own row. HGGD's cd variant on the same frames:

    A:cd picks a variant of a run; --variant picks one for both. The two records must share protocol and dataset. Whatever else differs between them (split, cameras, ground-truth-assisted inputs, post-processing, weights, code) is listed under differs, so that a difference in the numbers is not credited to the wrong cause.
